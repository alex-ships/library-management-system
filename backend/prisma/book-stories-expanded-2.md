# Expanded book stories, batch 2

Same format as before: each book starts with `=== BOOK: title ===`, and pages are separated by a line containing only `---`. This batch covers The Name of the Wind, Mistborn: The Final Empire, The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo and And Then There Were None. All text is original retelling.

=== BOOK: The Name of the Wind ===
# The Name of the Wind
by Patrick Rothfuss · Chapter-by-chapter story (spoilers)

## Part 1: The inn

On a quiet road, in a quiet village, stands the Waystone Inn. Its owner, a red-haired man who goes by Kote, wipes the bar, tells the regulars nothing about himself, and seems tired in a way that sleep cannot cure. His young helper Bast is friendly, strange and fiercely loyal. Something is wrong in the world. Rumours of dark creatures and spreading violence drift through the inn's gossip.

---

One night a monstrous spider-like creature called a scrael attacks a traveller outside the inn. Kote, who ought to be a mere innkeeper, deals with it in a way no innkeeper should be able to. A day later the injured traveller turns out to be a man named Devan Lochees, a scribe known as Chronicler, who recognises the innkeeper at last.

---

Chronicler tells Kote who he is: Kvothe, the most famous and most feared man in the world, a musician, magician, and killer of kings, the subject of a hundred half-true stories. Kvothe admits it. He says the stories are wrong, and he agrees to tell his true life story to Chronicler, over three days. This book is the first day, and Kvothe will tell it in his own voice.

## Part 2: The troupe

---

Kvothe begins with his childhood. He was born into the Edema Ruh, a travelling troupe of actors, singers and storytellers who go from town to town in painted wagons, and who are looked down on by many. His father Arliden is the troupe's leader, a gifted lyricist who is trying to write a great song. His mother Laurian is a noble's daughter who gave up her title for love, and who is the troupe's best performer.

---

Kvothe is a bright, restless boy, quick to learn songs, tricks and lines. He grows up in the back of a wagon with a lute in his hands, a family of performers around him, and the road under his wheels. Life is hard sometimes and wonderful at other times. He later says it was the happiest part of his life.

---

One summer a wandering arcanist named Abenthy joins the troupe. He is a sloppy, funny, brilliant man who teaches Kvothe the basics of sympathy, a kind of magic that links two objects through shared energy, and who makes the boy think hard about the nature of things. Abenthy also explains the idea of a "name", a hidden word that lets a person call the true nature of an object. Kvothe is hooked.

---

Abenthy talks about the University, a great school of arcane learning in the city of Imre, where the cleverest people in the world study. He says Kvothe is far too bright to waste his life on the road. He also leaves the troupe before winter, and Kvothe misses him, but he keeps practising sympathy by himself.

## Part 3: The song and the silence

---

Arliden begins to compose a song about the legendary Lanre, a story that is tied up with the Chandrian, seven mysterious figures who appear in old tales and children's rhymes. The Chandrian are said to be demons who destroy whole towns. They are always signalled by a sign: blue flame, rotting plants, or cold. Arliden's research into their history upsets his wife, who has a bad feeling about it.

---

Arliden refuses to stop. He tells Kvothe that he has found the truth hidden in the old stories, and that his song will be the greatest he has ever written. The troupe travels on through the woods, singing and joking, while the family fire burns each evening in a different clearing.

---

One day Kvothe goes off alone to practise, and returns to the camp at nightfall to find a scene of horror. The wagons are burnt. The horses have been left untouched. Everyone in the troupe is dead, killed silently and horribly, and the air is cold and strangely still. Kvothe finds his parents' bodies, and feels his world end.

---

He wanders in a daze for days, alone and numb with grief. Eventually he decides to do what his parents never could: to find out who killed them and why.

## Part 4: The city of beggars

---

Kvothe walks to the great city of Tarbean, a squalid port where the rich live in towers and the poor in alleys. He believes he can find information there. Instead, he finds hunger, cold and cruelty. He has no money, no friends, and no one to protect him. He sleeps in doorways and steals scraps from garbage bins.

---

For almost three years, Kvothe lives on the streets, a feral, starving child. He is beaten by older boys, robbed of his clothes, and nearly freezes in the winter. He learns to be quiet, quick and cunning, and he learns not to trust anyone. But he does not give up, and he is not lost. He keeps a small lute-string tucked away as a reminder of who he used to be.

---

Kvothe sometimes goes to listen to a storyteller named Skarpi, an old man who tells tales to the crowds. One day, Skarpi tells the story of Lanre and Selitos, a tale about a powerful man who made a terrible bargain to bring back his dead wife. Kvothe realises that this is the story his father was researching, and that it links to the Chandrian.

---

When Skarpi explains that the Chandrian are not just fairy-tale monsters, Kvothe's heart pounds. The old man talks about the history of the world, ancient wars, and a hidden city of forgotten power. Kvothe understands that there is a trail he can follow, and that the way to follow it lies in the University and its great library.

---

Kvothe decides to leave Tarbean. He scrapes together money by playing for coins and finally sets off on the road to Imre, a skinny teenager with a stolen cloak and a heavy heart. He has survived. Now he wants to learn, and then he wants revenge.

## Part 5: The University

---

Kvothe arrives at the University and is overwhelmed by the size of it: halls, libraries, workshops, hundreds of students with fine clothes and good manners. He has no money and very little formal education. To enter, he must pass an interview with the masters and pay tuition based on his ability.

---

At the admissions interview, Kvothe astonishes the masters with his wit and knowledge. He answers their questions, demonstrates his sympathy skills, and impresses them so much that he is admitted with a generous reduction in his fees. He is one of the youngest students ever accepted. His troubles are only beginning.

---

Kvothe quickly makes a friend and an enemy. The friend is Simmon, a gentle, cheerful student who shares his meals and his knowledge. Another friend is Wilem, a quiet Cealdish student with a sharp tongue. The enemy is Ambrose Jakis, a rich and arrogant noble who looks down on Kvothe and begins a long campaign to ruin him.

---

At the University, Kvothe studies under many masters. He learns artificery from Kilvin, a huge, gruff craftsman who values hard work above all. He learns about the human body at the Medica. He studies names under Elodin, a wild, unpredictable master who may be a genius or a madman, who teaches by jumping off roofs and sitting in odd places.

---

Kvothe spends hours in the Archives, a vast library with a strict rule: nobody may enter without permission. Kvothe breaks the rules and sneaks in to read books about the Chandrian. He is caught, and the Master Archivist bars him from the library for a time, which is a heavy blow, because so much of what he wants to learn is in those books.

## Part 6: Money, music and Denna

---

Money is a constant problem. Each term, Kvothe's tuition is due, and he scrambles to earn it. He makes sympathy lamps for sale, performs at a tavern, and takes on any odd job. Ambrose, who controls a great deal of influence, sabotages him whenever he can, and Kvothe's poverty becomes a weapon against him.

---

Kvothe plays music at the Eolian, a tavern where musicians perform for a chance to earn a coveted pipe, a badge that grants a student free entry and a place on the stage. Playing is his gift, and he is a master at it. His first performance silences the room, and he becomes a legend among the students.

---

He meets Denna, a mysterious, beautiful young woman with a lovely voice and a talent for disappearing, and she becomes a thread that runs through his story. They share a strange connection, full of laughter and half-truths, and Kvothe is soon in love. Denna refuses to explain where she comes from, whom she works for, or why she vanishes without warning. He will keep meeting her through the years, never quite sure where he stands.

---

Meanwhile, Kvothe's feud with Ambrose reaches new heights. Ambrose spreads lies about him. Kvothe retaliates with pranks and cunning. Their rivalry, which began as a simple clash of personalities, becomes bitter and personal, and it will shape the rest of his time at the University.

## Part 7: A dragon and a name

---

To earn money and pay his debts, Kvothe takes a dangerous job: hunting a draccus, a lizard-like monster that has been terrorising a farming village, and which is said to be a kind of dragon. He travels to the countryside and finds the creature is real and deadly, with a body that burns anything it touches.

---

Using his wits, his sympathy and a lot of nerve, Kvothe manages to trap the draccus and defeat it. The grateful villagers pay him, and the story of his victory spreads. He returns to the University with a reputation as a man who can do the impossible, and with some of his money troubles solved.

---

Back at the University, Elodin pushes Kvothe harder, trying to teach him the art of Naming, the most mysterious and powerful of the arts. Kvothe struggles. To name something, one must understand it so deeply that its name appears, and he cannot do it. He fails again and again.

---

Then, in a moment of calm, Kvothe listens to the wind and understands it. He feels it as a living thing, and a name rises in his mind. He speaks it aloud, and for a moment the wind obeys him, swirling around him in a great spiral. Elodin, watching, smiles. It is only the beginning of what Kvothe can do, and it comes at a cost he does not yet understand.

## Part 8: The end of the first day

---

Kvothe's story reaches the end of its first day. The reader, like Chronicler, knows that this clever, gifted, reckless boy has made enemies, lost his family, and chased a truth that may destroy him. Yet there is no tidy ending. He is still at the University, still searching, still poor, and still grieving.

---

In the inn, Kvothe pauses. He has told Chronicler the story of a boy who was swallowed by the dark and came out the other side. But the man who is telling it is a shadow of that boy, a quiet innkeeper who has hidden himself from the world. Why? What happened between the University and the inn? That is the story of the second and third days.

---

Bast watches his master with worry, knowing that the telling of the story is reopening old wounds. Chronicler writes furiously, trying to capture every word. Outside, the wind stirs, and the Waystone Inn waits for tomorrow, while the world beyond its walls grows darker.

=== BOOK: Mistborn: The Final Empire ===
# Mistborn: The Final Empire
by Brandon Sanderson · Chapter-by-chapter story (spoilers)

## Part 1: Ash and mist

For a thousand years, ash has fallen from the sky like grey snow. Plants are brown, the sun is red, and at night a strange mist covers the land. The immortal Lord Ruler rules the Final Empire from his palace, worshipped as a god. The skaa, the common people, are slaves, forced to work in the ash-choked fields and forges while the noble houses live in luxury.

---

In the city of Luthadel, a young skaa girl named Vin lives on the streets. She is a thief, a member of a small criminal crew led by a cruel man named Camon. Her brother Reen raised her to be wary of everyone: never trust anyone, he said, because everyone will betray you for money. Vin has scars, nightmares and a deep fear of being hurt again.

---

One night, Vin discovers she can do something unusual. When a nobleman tries to use a strange power on her emotions, she resists. She feels a fire inside her, and a pull in her stomach, as though some deep part of her could be lit. She does not understand it, and she keeps it secret.

---

Camon's crew is betrayed to the authorities, and Vin is nearly caught by the Steel Inquisitors, scary, spiked priests of the Lord Ruler. She escapes, and a stranger approaches her in the shadows. He is a tall, grinning man with a light, confident voice, who calls himself Kelsier. He tells Vin that she has a rare power, and that he can teach her to use it.

## Part 2: The survivor

---

Kelsier is a legend among the skaa, called the Survivor of Hathsin. He is the only person ever to escape the Lord Ruler's deadliest prison, the Pits of Hathsin, where thousands of slaves have died mining the metal atium. He lost the love of his life, Mare, to the Lord Ruler's cruelty, and he has come back from the pits with a burning desire to destroy the empire.

---

Kelsier explains allomancy, a magical art in which a person swallows small amounts of metal and "burns" them for power. Each metal gives a different ability: steel lets you push on nearby metal, iron lets you pull it, pewter gives strength, tin sharpens senses, brass soothes emotions, zinc inflames them. A rare few, called Mistborn, can burn every metal.

---

Vin, he tells her, is Mistborn. She is skeptical and suspicious, but also curious, and she cannot deny what she can feel. Kelsier offers her a place in his crew. He is putting together the most daring heist in history: to take down the Lord Ruler himself and free the skaa.

---

He introduces her to the crew: Dockson, the careful organiser; Ham, a gentle fighter who loves philosophical debate; Breeze, a smooth, charming Soother who manipulates people's emotions; Clubs, a grumpy old man who makes weapons; Spook, a young skaa lad; and Sazed, a Keeper from Terris who has memorised a vast amount of knowledge. For the first time in years, Vin begins to feel like she belongs.

## Part 3: The plan

---

Kelsier's plan has many layers. First, he wants to foment a skaa rebellion, so he will recruit an army from the starving, beaten workers of Luthadel. Second, he wants to steal the Lord Ruler's hidden stockpile of atium, so the noble houses will tear each other apart in a war. Third, he plans to kill the Lord Ruler, and to do that he needs Vin.

---

Vin trains hard. Kelsier teaches her to push and pull on metals, to fly through the air using coins, to fight with knives and with her mind. The mists, which most people fear, become her ally. She practises at night, leaping across rooftops, her cloak streaming behind her. For the first time in her life, she feels powerful.

---

But Kelsier also wants Vin to learn to trust. He does this by giving her small tasks and showing her kindness. She is still wary, but she is learning. When he tells her the truth about his own past, and his grief, she begins to see him as more than a mysterious leader. He is a broken man holding himself together with hope.

---

The crew needs Vin for a more delicate mission, too. She must go undercover in the noble society, pretending to be the niece of a minor lord, so she can gather information about the great houses, their secrets and their weaknesses. It means she will have to wear dresses, dance at balls, and pretend to be someone she is not.

## Part 4: Lady Valette

---

Vin takes the name Valette Renoux and enters the world of the nobility. She is dazzled and terrified by the opulence, the elaborate customs, and the cruelty hidden behind politeness. The nobles treat the skaa as animals, and Vin has to smile through it. Sazed helps her to learn etiquette, and she gradually learns to move and speak like a lady.

---

At her first ball, she meets Elend Venture, the heir of a powerful noble house. He is awkward, bookish and idealistic, and he is more interested in philosophy than in politics. He reads books about government and justice, and he cares about the suffering of the skaa. He is nothing like the other nobles, and Vin is drawn to him.

---

Elend's father, Lord Straff Venture, is a cold, ambitious man who has little patience for his son's dreams. He wants Elend to be a strong leader, ruthless and practical. Elend feels like a failure in his father's eyes. Vin sees how lonely he is, and in him she finds someone who also feels out of place.

---

As Vin spends more time with the nobility, she senses that something is off. Some of the great houses are preparing for war. Others are hoarding atium. And in the shadows, the Steel Inquisitors watch. She reports to Kelsier, and the crew uses her information to refine their plan. Vin realises that she is doing something important, and that she cares about the people she is helping.

## Part 5: The skaa army

---

Meanwhile, Kelsier raises an army of skaa. He goes to the plantations, finds men who hate the Lord Ruler, and tells them the truth: the empire's power depends on their fear. He wins them over with his charm and his courage. The army grows, hidden in the caves under the city, drilled by Ham and others.

---

Kelsier also sabotages the nobles in clever ways, using forged documents, bribes and rumour. He turns noble houses against each other, destroying alliances and trust. The nobility, which had thought itself invincible, begins to fracture. The great houses mobilise their own soldiers, and tension builds.

---

The crew also learns that the Lord Ruler is not what he seems. He appears to be immortal and all-powerful, but he has secrets. Sazed, who has studied the old religions of Terris, begins to suspect that the Lord Ruler's power has a source, and that the stories he tells about his origin are lies.

---

Vin's feelings for Elend grow, even as she keeps her secret. She is torn between her loyalty to Kelsier and her fondness for the young noble. She wants to tell Elend the truth, but she knows it would endanger everyone. At one point, she nearly confesses, and then holds back. She does not yet know that she will have to choose.

## Part 6: The rebellion

---

The time comes for the rebellion. Kelsier gives a great speech to his army, promising that they will take Luthadel, and that the skaa will be free. The army is nervous but determined. The crew puts the final pieces in place: they have a plan to get into the Lord Ruler's palace, to take the atium and to kill the god-king.

---

But the Lord Ruler is ready. As the rebellion begins, the Steel Inquisitors attack. Kelsier fights them with all his skill, and Vin fights beside him. Many skaa die in the fighting. In the chaos, the Lord Ruler himself appears, descending from his palace like a vision of doom, and he walks calmly through the battle.

---

Kelsier faces the Lord Ruler in a final confrontation. He burns all his metals, summons every trick he knows, and hits the Lord Ruler with everything he has. It is not enough. The Lord Ruler cuts him down, and Kelsier dies in the street, in front of the people he was trying to save. Vin, watching from a rooftop, is crushed.

---

But Kelsier had planned for this. He had told the skaa that if he died, they must not despair, because a martyr's death would give them courage. And so it does. News of his death spreads through the city, and the skaa rise in fury. His death, unexpectedly, accomplishes what his life could not.

## Part 7: The Well

---

Vin, broken by grief, finds that Kelsier left her a final gift. He and Sazed had pieced together a secret about the Lord Ruler: his apparent immortality is not a god's gift but a trick, built on power stolen from the people of Terris. If Vin can face him, the secret may be enough to end his reign.

---

She goes to the Lord Ruler's palace, a vast, eerie fortress. Inside, the halls are silent and cold. She fights Inquisitors, hides in shadows, and burns metals to survive. When she finally finds the Lord Ruler, he is not what she expected: a calm, weary man who speaks to her as an equal.

---

He tells Vin that he, too, once believed in a better world. A thousand years ago he went to a place called the Well of Ascension and took a power that was never meant for one man, and he has been paying for it ever since. He thinks he is saving humanity by ruling it. Vin disagrees. In the fight that follows, she uses everything she has learned, including a trick Kelsier taught her, to defeat him.

---

The Lord Ruler dies. His empire, held together by his power and the people's fear, begins to collapse. Vin, exhausted and numb, stumbles out of the palace to find the city in uproar.

## Part 8: The new age

---

Luthadel is in chaos. The skaa have taken the streets, the noble houses are fleeing, and the armies of Straff Venture are camped outside the walls. Elend, who has been working to help the skaa, steps up to lead. He is not a warrior or a schemer, but he believes in justice and in the idea that people can rule themselves.

---

Vin, who has lost so much, finds in Elend someone she can trust. They talk about the future, about Kelsier, about the hope that the skaa and nobles might find a way to live together. Neither of them knows how to build a new world. But for the first time in a thousand years, the question is open.

---

The book ends with a quiet moment. Vin stands on a rooftop under the mist, remembering Kelsier, who taught her to trust and to dream. The ash still falls, but a little more lightly. The empire is over, and its people, free but frightened, must decide what to build in its place. And Vin, a girl who once trusted no one, is ready to find out.

=== BOOK: The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo ===
# The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo
by Stieg Larsson · Chapter-by-chapter story (spoilers)

## Part 1: The disgraced journalist

Mikael Blomkvist is a well-known Swedish journalist and co-owner of the magazine Millennium, famous for exposing corruption. He has just lost a libel case against Hans-Erik Wennerström, a powerful financier whom he accused, on the strength of a tip, of illegal arms deals. The evidence was a trap, and Mikael walked into it. He is sentenced to three months in prison and fined heavily.

---

Humiliated, Mikael resigns as editor of Millennium to protect the magazine from the scandal. His longtime colleague and lover, Erika Berger, tries to keep things running. Mikael feels he has failed everyone, and he is not sure he will ever work as a journalist again. He needs a break, and a way to restore his name.

---

At this low point, a lawyer approaches him with an unusual offer. An elderly industrialist, Henrik Vanger, wants to hire him for a year to write a history of the Vanger family. The pay is generous, and Henrik promises that in return, he will give Mikael proof that Wennerström is guilty. Mikael accepts, though he suspects there is more to the job than he's been told.

## Part 2: The girl with the tattoo

---

Meanwhile, in Stockholm, a young woman named Lisbeth Salander works as a freelance investigator for a security firm. She is small, pale and pierced, with a dragon tattoo on her shoulder. She dresses in black, speaks little and trusts no one. She is also a brilliant hacker and has a photographic memory. People underestimate her, and she lets them.

---

Lisbeth was declared legally incompetent as a teenager and is under the guardianship of the state. When her kind old guardian is replaced by a lawyer named Nils Bjurman, her life takes a dark turn. Bjurman controls her money and demands sexual favours in return for access to her own funds. He assaults her cruelly, and Lisbeth is deeply hurt.

---

But Lisbeth is not a victim. She plans her revenge with cold precision. On her next visit, she secretly films Bjurman's abuse with a hidden camera. She returns, overpowers him, and brands his stomach with a tattoo that tells the world what he is. She then uses the footage to force him to give her full control of her life. Bjurman is terrified of her.

---

Lisbeth's boss, Dragan Armansky, asked her to investigate Mikael Blomkvist for the Vanger family. She produced a thorough report. Though she does not know it, her path is about to cross with his. She has been fascinated by Mikael's case, and feels certain that he was set up.

## Part 3: The island

---

Mikael travels to Hedestad and the remote island of Hedeby, a village mainly owned by the Vanger family. The Vangers are a large, wealthy and unhappy clan, many of whom hate each other. Some are Nazi sympathisers, others are merely cold and greedy. Mikael lives in a small cottage on the island and begins his research.

---

Henrik finally tells him the real job. In 1966, his beloved great-niece Harriet Vanger vanished on the island during a family gathering. A bridge to the mainland was blocked by an accident, so she could not have left, yet she was never found. Henrik believes she was murdered by someone in his own family, and for forty years every birthday he has received a framed pressed flower, which he thinks is the killer's cruel joke.

---

Mikael is sceptical, but he agrees to look at the case. He reads old police files, interviews family members and studies photographs of the day Harriet disappeared. The Vangers are suspicious and hostile. A retired police inspector who worked the original case gives him background, and a family member named Cecilia Vanger, a teacher, begins a secret relationship with him.

---

He finds an interesting clue in a photo taken at a parade in Hedestad on the day of the disappearance. In it, Harriet looks at something, or someone, in the crowd, and her face changes. Mikael tracks down the photographer and begins to piece together what she may have seen. But he needs help, and he is stuck.

## Part 4: The partnership

---

Lisbeth is brought into the case when Mikael asks for a researcher to help him. Mikael discovers, with some surprise, that the girl who investigated him knows his whole life. He finds her odd and difficult, but she is an extraordinary researcher. They begin to work together, with Lisbeth searching databases and Mikael on the ground.

---

Lisbeth finds a connection that the police missed: Harriet's diary contains a list of five names and numbers that looked meaningless. She realises that these correspond to Bible verses, and that each verse is linked to the murders of young women, in a pattern of killings across Sweden stretching back to the 1940s.

---

The murders were brutal and ritualistic, committed against women who had Jewish names or ties. Mikael and Lisbeth begin to suspect that a serial killer, possibly within the Vanger family, has been active for decades, and that Harriet found out. The case turns from a missing-person mystery into something much darker.

---

As they work, Lisbeth and Mikael grow close. Their relationship is not simple: she is guarded, he is warm and patient, and they treat each other with a strange respect. They sleep together, and then, in her typical way, she says nothing about it afterward. For Lisbeth, who never lets anyone in, it is a significant step.

## Part 5: The killer

---

The trail leads to Martin Vanger, the family's polished, charming CEO, who runs the company and has been kind to Mikael. His father, Gottfried, an alcoholic and Nazi sympathiser, had a dark and abusive relationship with his children, and Martin learned from him. Mikael realises that Martin has been killing women for years, as his father did before him.

---

Mikael goes to Martin's house to discuss his suspicions, naively believing that he is in no danger. Martin drugs him and chains him in a hidden basement, a soundproofed room where he has tortured and killed women. Martin, calm and proud, tells Mikael everything, enjoying the sense of power he has over his captive.

---

Martin admits that he and his father abused Harriet when she was a girl. But he swears he does not know what became of her on the day she vanished. Her disappearance remains a mystery even to him. As he prepares to kill Mikael, Lisbeth, who has been watching, bursts in and attacks him with a golf club.

---

Martin flees in his car, and in a desperate, high-speed chase Lisbeth follows on her motorcycle. Martin loses control of his car and crashes into a truck, dying in the flames. Lisbeth nurses Mikael back to health. Together, they have solved one of Sweden's darkest mysteries, though one piece is still missing: what happened to Harriet?

## Part 6: Harriet

---

Mikael and Lisbeth find that Harriet is not dead. Using Lisbeth's skills, they discover that a woman living in Australia under the name Anita Vanger is in fact Harriet. She had been helped to flee by her cousin Anita and has lived abroad ever since, quietly running a sheep farm, haunted by her past.

---

Mikael flies to Australia, finds Harriet, and persuades her to return to Sweden. Harriet tells him the truth: she slipped away from the island during the chaos on the day of the parade. She had been abused by her father and brother, and she fled to save herself. She had never known that Martin was a serial killer, and she is shaken by the news of his crimes.

---

Harriet returns to Hedestad and is reunited with Henrik, who is overjoyed to see her alive. She takes control of the family company, in place of her dead brother. The mystery that Henrik spent forty years brooding over is finally solved, though the price is a dreadful family history that must now be faced.

## Part 7: Wennerström

---

Henrik, true to his word, gives Mikael what he promised. But the dossier on Wennerström is useless, mostly old gossip, and Mikael feels cheated. Henrik tells him he had always known the dossier was thin, but that he needed Mikael's help with Harriet. Mikael is bitter and angry, and he feels he has wasted a year.

---

Lisbeth, however, has a plan of her own. Having hacked into Wennerström's computer and bank accounts, she has discovered solid evidence of his illegal dealings, including money laundering and fraud. She gives it to Mikael, who is amazed. Armed with the proof, he writes a blistering exposé for Millennium, and the story is a sensation.

---

Wennerström's empire collapses, and he is forced to flee. Mikael's reputation is restored, and Millennium becomes more successful than ever. But Lisbeth has done something else too: while hacking Wennerström's accounts, she quietly transfers a vast fortune into her own. She has taken billions of kronor from a criminal, and she is now very rich.

---

In the final scene, Lisbeth goes to find Mikael, carrying a gift. She stands outside his building and sees him with Erika, laughing and affectionate. She realises she is not part of this picture. She turns away, drops the gift into a bin, and walks off into the Stockholm evening, alone, her feelings buried once more. She is still the girl with the dragon tattoo, and she has changed more than she will admit.

=== BOOK: And Then There Were None ===
# And Then There Were None
by Agatha Christie · Chapter-by-chapter story (spoilers)

## Part 1: The invitations

In the summer of 1939, ten strangers travel to a small island off the Devon coast. Each has received a puzzling invitation. Judge Wargrave has been asked to join a friend, the young Vera Claythorne has been offered a job as a secretary, and others have been promised a holiday, a business meeting or an old acquaintance. A boatman takes them to the island, and the first sign that something is odd is that nobody can say who owns it.

---

The guests are an eclectic group. Besides the judge and Vera, there are General Macarthur, a retired army officer; Dr Armstrong, a respectable physician; Emily Brent, a stern, devout spinster; Anthony Marston, a careless young man; William Blore, a former police inspector; Philip Lombard, a soldier of fortune; and two servants, Mr and Mrs Rogers, who arrived earlier to run the house.

---

The island, called Soldier Island, is a rocky outcrop with a sleek modern house. In each bedroom hangs a framed nursery rhyme about ten little soldier boys, each of whom dies in a different way, until none are left. On the dining table are ten little china soldier figures. The guests think it is a curious bit of decoration.

---

After dinner, a mysterious voice booms out from a gramophone record that Mr Rogers has been told to play. It accuses each of the ten people of having committed murder, in some way that the law could not touch. The guests are shocked, outraged and afraid. Most deny the charges. A few, privately, are terrified, because the accusation is true.

## Part 2: The first deaths

---

The guests try to work out who has brought them here. The invitations were signed by a Mr U. N. Owen, but no one has met him. The servants say they were hired by a couple they have never seen. There is no boat on the island, and the boatman will not return until the next day.

---

Anthony Marston is the first to die. After the dinner, he takes a drink and suddenly chokes, gasping for air, and falls dead. The doctor finds that he was poisoned with cyanide. It seems impossible, but it is certain. Soon afterwards, someone notices that one of the little china soldiers on the dining table is missing.

---

The next morning, Mrs Rogers is found dead in her bed, apparently from an overdose of a sleeping drug. Nobody saw her take it. Her husband is distraught. A second china soldier has disappeared. The rhyme suggests that the deaths are following its words, and the guests begin to feel horror creeping in.

---

A storm blows in, and the island is cut off from the mainland. General Macarthur, the old soldier, calmly says that he has been waiting for death for years, and sits by himself on the shore. When the others go to look for him, they find him dead, struck from behind. A third soldier vanishes.

## Part 3: The search

---

The remaining seven guests realise that the murderer must be one of them. They search the island from end to end, looking for a hidden killer, but there is nothing: only rocks, sea, and a small house. They search each other's rooms, take away anything dangerous and lock up the medicine and the guns.

---

Suspicion tears the group apart. Blore, the former detective, thinks that the killer is the doctor. Lombard thinks it is Blore. Vera trusts no one. They argue and quarrel, each unwilling to stay alone but unable to trust the others. They agree to stay together, and to watch each other, for the night.

---

During the night, Mr Rogers goes out to chop wood and is found dead the next morning, killed by an axe blow to the head. Another soldier is missing. The survivors, now six in number, are stunned. Miss Brent, strong in her religious faith, calmly states that they are all being punished for their sins.

---

Miss Brent is the next to die, poisoned with a hypodermic syringe while sitting in the dining room. Her death reveals that the killer is crafty and bold, striking right under their noses. The guests begin to doubt even their own memories. Each wonders who will be next, and whether they will survive the night.

## Part 4: The judge

Judge Wargrave, an old man with a sharp mind, takes charge. He suggests that they gather in one room and keep watch together. Five soldiers have vanished from the table, and only five people are left alive, the judge among them. Nobody sleeps well that night.

---

In the morning, the judge is found dead in the drawing room. He sits in his chair, wearing a judge's wig and a red robe, and a bullet hole sits in the centre of his forehead. Dr Armstrong examines him and confirms that he is dead. The survivors, now four in number, are shaken, and each of them privately suspects the other three.

---

During the night, Dr Armstrong goes out alone and does not come back. The others search the island and find nothing. Another soldier has disappeared from the table. Later his body washes up on the shore. He has drowned, and nobody can explain how he got to the water.

---

Then Blore is killed, crushed by a large marble clock that falls from a window. Lombard and Vera, the last two, retreat to a high rock and watch the sea. The storm has passed, but the boat has still not come. The air is thick with fear, and each of them now suspects the other.

## Part 5: The last two

---

Lombard and Vera sit and watch each other warily. Lombard, who has a revolver that he had hidden, and Vera, who has not, begin to circle each other. Vera has reason to be afraid: she, too, is guilty of a death, that of a small child she was supposed to be looking after, and she has never confessed.

---

Eventually, in a tense scene, Vera manages to get hold of Lombard's gun. She shoots him dead, convinced that he is the killer. She is alone on the island, and she thinks that the nightmare is over. Exhausted and shaking, she goes back to the house, where she finds, in her bedroom, a noose hanging from a hook.

---

Haunted by the voice that accused her, and by the faces of the dead, Vera sees the noose as an invitation. She had been driven by guilt for years, and now there is no escape. She climbs on a chair, puts her head in the noose, and kicks the chair away. The last soldier is gone.

## Part 6: The confession

---

When the police arrive, they find ten bodies and a baffling puzzle. All the victims are accounted for, and none could have killed the others. There is no one else on the island. The only clue is the rhyme and the dead china figures. The case becomes a famous mystery that nobody can solve.

---

Months later, a fishing boat finds a bottle floating at sea, with a sealed manuscript inside. It is a confession written by Judge Wargrave. He explains that he has always loved justice, and also had a secret desire to kill. As a judge, he sent many people to the gallows, but he longed to commit a murder himself and get away with it.

---

He chose ten people who had escaped justice and lured them to the island under the name of U. N. Owen. He poisoned Marston and Mrs Rogers, and the others he killed in turn. He faked his own death, with the help of Dr Armstrong, whom he fooled, and then he killed the doctor. He arranged things so that Vera would hang herself.

---

Wargrave closes by describing what he did after Vera's death: he shot himself, in a way that would look like murder, leaving his body to be discovered. The puzzle he built is perfect, and he knows no one will solve it unless they read his words. He wrote the confession out of a last, strange vanity, and entrusted it to the sea, hoping someone might find it and understand.
