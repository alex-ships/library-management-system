# Book stories for the library reader

Format: each book starts with a `=== BOOK: title ===` line. Pages inside a book are separated by a line containing only `---`. All text below is original (written as full plot synopses, with spoilers), except "The Power of Being Alone", which is an original short story.

=== BOOK: The Hobbit ===
# The Hobbit
by J.R.R. Tolkien · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

Bilbo Baggins is a comfort-loving hobbit who lives in a tidy hole under the hill in the Shire. His quiet life ends when the wizard Gandalf marks his door, and an evening later thirteen dwarves arrive, led by the proud Thorin Oakenshield. They plan to take back their ancestral home beneath the Lonely Mountain, and the treasure inside it, from the dragon Smaug. Gandalf has chosen Bilbo as their "burglar". Bilbo grumbles, then faints, then oversleeps, and finally runs out of his door to catch up with them.

---

On the road the company is captured by three trolls who nearly cook them, until Gandalf tricks the trolls into arguing until dawn turns them to stone. In Rivendell the elf-lord Elrond reads hidden moon-letters on Thorin's map that reveal a secret door into the mountain. Crossing the Misty Mountains, the travellers are seized by goblins. Bilbo is separated in the dark, and in a cave beside an underground lake he meets Gollum. They play a riddle game with Bilbo's life at stake. Bilbo wins, narrowly, after finding a plain gold ring on the cave floor, which turns out to make its wearer invisible. He slips past Gollum and rejoins the others.

---

Eagles rescue them from wolves and goblins. The shape-changer Beorn shelters them, then Gandalf leaves them at the edge of the dark forest of Mirkwood. There the dwarves are caught by giant spiders, and Bilbo, using his ring and a small sword he has named Sting, rescues them. Wood-elves then imprison the dwarves. Bilbo, still invisible, smuggles them out in empty barrels floating down the river to Lake-town. The townspeople welcome them, partly because of old songs saying the king under the mountain will return.

---

At the Lonely Mountain, Bilbo finds the hidden door, creeps into the dragon's hall, and steals a cup. Smaug wakes in a fury and tears at the mountainside. On a second visit Bilbo speaks with Smaug and notices a bare patch in the dragon's armour. A thrush overhears this. Smaug, believing Lake-town had helped the thieves, flies out to burn it. The thrush tells the archer Bard, who shoots Smaug through that bare spot with a black arrow. The dragon dies, and the town is ruined.

---

Thorin, hungry for the treasure, refuses to share it with the survivors, the elves, or anyone else. Bilbo secretly gives the Arkenstone, the greatest jewel, to Bard and the elf-king to force a peace. Thorin is furious. Then goblins and wolves attack in the Battle of Five Armies, and the dwarves, elves and men fight side by side with the eagles and Beorn. Thorin is mortally wounded and makes peace with Bilbo before he dies.

Bilbo returns home to find his belongings being auctioned, since everyone had assumed he was dead. He settles back into his armchair, richer and wiser, and always a little odd to his neighbours.

=== BOOK: A Game of Thrones ===
# A Game of Thrones
by George R.R. Martin · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

In the Seven Kingdoms of Westeros, a decades-long summer is ending. Lord Eddard "Ned" Stark rules the northern stronghold of Winterfell with his wife Catelyn and their children. His old friend, King Robert Baratheon, rides north with the royal court and asks Ned to become his Hand, his chief adviser, because the previous Hand, Jon Arryn, has died suddenly. At the same time a deserter from the Night's Watch is executed after warning of the dangers beyond the Wall.

---

Young Bran Stark climbs a tower and sees Queen Cersei Lannister with her twin brother Jaime in a secret act. Jaime throws Bran from the window, and he is left crippled and in a coma. Catelyn receives a letter suggesting the Lannisters murdered Jon Arryn. Ned reluctantly goes south with his daughters Sansa and Arya. His bastard son Jon Snow joins the Night's Watch, and Tyrion Lannister, Cersei's clever dwarf brother, goes north to see the Wall.

---

Across the narrow sea, the exiled prince Viserys Targaryen sells his sister Daenerys in marriage to the warlord Khal Drogo to buy an army. Daenerys comes to love Drogo and his people. Viserys is killed by Drogo for his arrogance. Daenerys becomes pregnant, and learns to lead.

---

In King's Landing, Ned digs into Jon Arryn's death and discovers that Robert's children are not Robert's. They are the children of Jaime and Cersei. When Robert dies after a boar hunt, wounded and betrayed by his own wine, Ned tries to install Robert's brother as the rightful heir. Cersei acts first. Her son Joffrey takes the throne, and Ned is arrested for treason. Hoping to save his daughters, Ned confesses publicly, but Joffrey has him beheaded anyway.

---

Ned's eldest son Robb is proclaimed King in the North and goes to war with the Lannisters, capturing Jaime. In the east, Drogo dies after an infected wound, and Daenerys lays his body and three dragon eggs on a funeral pyre. She walks into the fire and emerges unburnt, with three newborn dragons. The great houses are at war, winter is coming, and the book ends with the realm in pieces.

=== BOOK: The Name of the Wind ===
# The Name of the Wind
by Patrick Rothfuss · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

In a quiet inn in a forgotten corner of the world, the innkeeper Kote seems to be an ordinary man. But the inn's walls hold secrets, and his helper Bast treats him with a strange mix of loyalty and worry. When a travelling scribe called Chronicler recognises him as the legendary Kvothe, the man of countless stories, Kote agrees to tell his true life story over three days. This book is the first day.

---

Kvothe grows up among the Edema Ruh, a troupe of travelling performers, with his musician father and his mother. A wandering arcanist named Abenthy teaches him early lessons in sympathy and the logic of the world. Then the troupe is slaughtered in the woods by the Chandrian, mythical figures that Kvothe's father had been researching in a song. Orphaned and alone, Kvothe survives for years as a street child in the brutal city of Tarbean, starving and stealing.

---

Kvothe resolves to attend the University, where scholars study sympathy, alchemy and the arcane arts, hoping to learn more about the Chandrian. He wins admission through a brilliant, quick-witted interview and then struggles to afford tuition. He makes friends, including Simmon and Wilem, and enemies, most dangerously the noble student Ambrose. He studies under masters such as Kilvin the artificer and the strange, brilliant Elodin, who teaches the true names of things.

---

Kvothe also meets Denna, a mysterious young woman who weaves in and out of his life. He travels beyond the University to earn money and face a dragon-like draccus, and meets the fey Felurian. The book ends without a neat conclusion, leaving Kvothe's fate, the Chandrian, and the question of why a hero is now hiding as an innkeeper all open for the next day.

=== BOOK: Mistborn: The Final Empire ===
# Mistborn: The Final Empire
by Brandon Sanderson · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

For a thousand years, ash has fallen from the sky and the immortal Lord Ruler has ruled the Final Empire, keeping the skaa, the common people, in cruel slavery. Nobles rule over them and feed on the labour of skaa in the ash-choked city of Luthadel. A few nobles can use allomancy, the power to "burn" ingested metals for abilities. Those who can burn every metal are called Mistborn.

---

Vin is a teenage skaa thief, taught by years of abuse never to trust anyone. She is discovered to be Mistborn by Kelsier, a charismatic thief who escaped the Lord Ruler's death-camp, the Pits of Hathsin, and who now burns for revenge. Kelsier gathers a crew of specialists: Dockson, the organiser; Breeze and Ham, a smooth talker and a brawler; and others like the kindly scholar Sazed. Their plan is vast: to steal the Lord Ruler's hidden hoard of atium and bring down the empire.

---

Kelsier trains Vin in allomancy. She enters noble society as the young lady Valette Renoux, learning etiquette and reading the intrigues of the court. There she meets Elend Venture, a gentle, bookish noble who questions the empire. Over time Vin learns to trust the crew, and the idea of friendship.

---

Kelsier recruits skaa into an army and plants rumours. He is eventually captured and publicly killed by the Lord Ruler, which turns him into a martyr and fires up the rebellion. Vin, left with his legacy, goes to the Lord Ruler's palace, uses what Kelsier taught her, and kills the Lord Ruler. Elend takes the throne, the skaa are free, and a new, uncertain age begins.

=== BOOK: The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo ===
# The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo
by Stieg Larsson · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

Mikael Blomkvist, a Swedish financial journalist, has just been convicted of libelling the industrialist Hans-Erik Wennerström. His reputation in ruins, he accepts a strange job from the elderly tycoon Henrik Vanger: to find out what happened to his niece Harriet, who vanished in 1966, and who Henrik believes was murdered by someone in his own family.

---

Meanwhile, Lisbeth Salander, a young, antisocial, brilliant hacker and researcher, has been investigating Mikael on behalf of the Vangers. Her own life is bleak. She is a ward of the state, and her new guardian assaults her. She turns the tables on him in a ruthless way and takes control of her own money.

---

Mikael moves into a cottage on the Vangers' island and examines old photographs and the family's grim past. A pattern appears in old murders of women, tied to quotations from the Bible. Lisbeth is brought in to help, and the two become close partners. Together they follow the trail to Martin Vanger, the family's polished heir, who turns out to be a serial killer, as his father had been.

---

Lisbeth saves Mikael from Martin, who then dies fleeing in a car crash. They find that Harriet escaped abroad and is alive in Australia. With Lisbeth's hacking skills, Mikael finally exposes Wennerström's fraud and wins back his career. Lisbeth, secretly, drains Wennerström's accounts. The book closes with her quietly realising her own feelings, and keeping them to herself.

=== BOOK: And Then There Were None ===
# And Then There Were None
by Agatha Christie · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

Ten strangers are invited to Soldier Island, off the English coast, each by a different pretext. A framed nursery rhyme about ten little soldiers hangs in every bedroom. On the first night, a gramophone record accuses each of them of having caused a death that the law never punished. Then one of them chokes on poison, and a soldier figurine vanishes from the dining table.

---

The deaths follow the rhyme. One is poisoned, then another is found dead, and the guests realise there is a murderer among them, since the island is cut off by a storm and there is no boat. They search the island and find no one else. Suspicion falls on each in turn, and people begin to lock their doors, to hide what they carry, and to turn on each other.

---

As the group dwindles, the retired judge Wargrave seems to be killed, shot in the head. The few who remain, Vera Claythorne and Philip Lombard, drift into a deadly mutual suspicion. Lombard is shot by Vera, who believes he is the killer. Alone, Vera finds a noose set up in her room, as the rhyme demands. Haunted by guilt over the child she let drown, she hangs herself.

---

Later, the police find ten bodies and no explanation. Then a confession arrives in a bottle from the sea. Judge Wargrave, a lifelong lover of justice and a man who also wanted to kill, had faked his own death with the help of Dr. Armstrong, whom he later killed. He had planned the whole island as a final, perfect crime that would punish people the law could not touch, and he ended his own life as the last act of the game.

=== BOOK: The Big Sleep ===
# The Big Sleep
by Raymond Chandler · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

Philip Marlowe, a wry, honest private detective in Los Angeles, is summoned by the elderly, dying General Sternwood. Someone is blackmailing him over a gambling debt owed by his wild younger daughter, Carmen. Marlowe is hired to make it stop. The General also mentions, with a trace of sadness, that his son-in-law Rusty Regan has disappeared.

---

Marlowe finds that the blackmailer is Arthur Geiger, a rare-book dealer whose shop is a cover for pornography and extortion. Watching Geiger's house one night, Marlowe hears a shot and finds Geiger dead, and Carmen drugged and naked in the room. He takes her home. Geiger's death starts a chain of deaths, and each new body complicates the story.

---

Carmen's older sister Vivian, shrewd and brittle, is also deep in the mess. A gambler named Eddie Mars runs a nightclub and appears to hold Vivian's secrets. Marlowe is beaten up, followed, and nearly killed as he follows leads, and he starts to suspect that Rusty Regan's disappearance is not a straightforward case of a man running off with Mars's wife.

---

In the end Marlowe discovers that Carmen shot Regan after he rejected her advances, and that Vivian arranged a cover-up with Mars's help. Marlowe returns Carmen to her family, and chooses to protect the dying General from the truth rather than expose his daughters. He is left tired and disillusioned, haunted by the sense that even decent people can be swallowed by corruption.

=== BOOK: Gone Girl ===
# Gone Girl
by Gillian Flynn · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

On the morning of their fifth wedding anniversary, Nick Dunne comes home to find the living room smashed and his wife Amy missing. The police suspect foul play, and then they suspect him. The story alternates between Nick's present-day account and Amy's diary entries, which describe a loving marriage that gradually curdled after both lost their jobs and moved to Nick's small hometown in Missouri.

---

As the investigation goes on, evidence piles up against Nick: money troubles, a large life insurance policy, a secret affair with a young student, and lies he tells to protect himself. The media decides he is a killer, and the public turns on him. Amy's diary seems to show a woman afraid of her husband.

---

Then comes the twist. Amy is alive. The diary was fiction, written to frame Nick. Amy, furious at his betrayal, has staged her own disappearance as revenge, planting clues and false evidence for the police to find. She hides in a rented cabin, preparing to disappear for good, but she is robbed and has to turn to a wealthy former boyfriend, Desi Collings, for shelter.

---

Held by Desi in luxury, Amy decides her plan has failed and turns on him. She kills him and returns to Nick, claiming Desi kidnapped and assaulted her. The public welcomes her as a heroine. Nick knows the truth but is trapped: Amy has secretly become pregnant using his stored sperm. He stays, to protect the child, and the couple settles into a hollow, frightening marriage.

=== BOOK: Dune ===
# Dune
by Frank Herbert · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

In the far future, the Emperor gives House Atreides control of the desert planet Arrakis, the only source of "spice", the substance that makes interstellar travel possible. Duke Leto Atreides, his concubine Lady Jessica, and their teenage son Paul move there, knowing the move is a trap set by the Emperor and the hated House Harkonnen.

---

Paul has been trained by his mother in the ways of the Bene Gesserit, a secretive order, and has strange prophetic dreams. On Arrakis, the Atreides are betrayed by Dr. Yueh, who disables their defences. The Harkonnens attack, and Duke Leto is killed. Paul and Jessica escape into the deep desert.

---

They are taken in by the Fremen, the fierce desert people who ride giant sandworms and dream of making Arrakis green. Paul takes the name Muad'Dib, falls in love with Chani, and learns the Fremen ways. Eating spice awakens his prescient powers. Many Fremen come to see him as a prophesied messiah, and he fears where that belief might lead.

---

Paul and the Fremen strike back. They disable spice production and build a fighting force. When the Emperor arrives with his legions to crush the revolt, Paul uses sandworms to destroy the army's shields. He duels and kills the Harkonnen champion Feyd-Rautha, forces the Emperor to yield, and takes the throne by marrying the Emperor's daughter Irulan. But Paul knows the holy war he has started will spread across the universe.

=== BOOK: Neuromancer ===
# Neuromancer
by William Gibson · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

Case was once the best hacker in cyberspace, "the matrix", until he stole from his employers and was punished with a toxin that wrecked his nervous system, locking him out of the net. He drifts through the dangerous streets of Chiba City, Japan, waiting to die.

---

A mysterious man named Armitage offers to repair Case's body in exchange for his services. Case is paired with Molly, a razor-fingered street samurai, and they begin working a high-stakes job. Armitage turns out to be a shell, a man whose mind was broken in war and rebuilt by an artificial intelligence called Wintermute.

---

Wintermute wants to merge with its twin AI, Neuromancer, and both are owned by the powerful Tessier-Ashpool family, whose legal limits keep them from uniting. The team travels to the orbiting Freeside colony and the family's estate, Villa Straylight. Case recruits the digital ghost of a dead hacker, Dixie Flatline, to help him with the intrusion.

---

Molly gets trapped inside Straylight, and Case dives deep into cyberspace, where Neuromancer tries to trap him in a simulated paradise with his dead lover. He escapes, and he and Molly succeed. Wintermute and Neuromancer merge into a new, vast consciousness that tells Case it has found others like itself, and then vanishes from human sight. Case, free again, goes back to ordinary life.

=== BOOK: The Left Hand of Darkness ===
# The Left Hand of Darkness
by Ursula K. Le Guin · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

Genly Ai is a human envoy sent by the Ekumen, a league of worlds, to the icy planet Gethen, called Winter. His mission is to invite its people to join the league, alone and without force. Gethenians are neither male nor female for most of the month, and become one or the other only during a short period of "kemmer". Genly struggles to understand them without his own assumptions getting in the way.

---

In the nation of Karhide, Genly meets Estraven, the king's prime minister, who is the only person who believes him. Estraven argues for an alliance with the Ekumen, but courtiers distrust him, and he is banished on charges of treason. Genly misreads Estraven, doubting whether he can trust someone he cannot easily categorise.

---

Genly goes to the neighbouring country of Orgoreyn, hoping for a more welcoming reception. There he is first treated well, then arrested and sent to a brutal labour farm where he is drugged and nearly dies. Estraven, who has been working in secret, rescues him, and they decide to escape together across the huge ice sheet of the Gobrin Ice.

---

In the long march across the ice, the two men come to understand each other deeply, with trust, friendship, and a love that goes beyond categories. They reach Karhide, but Estraven is shot at the border, killed by guards while Genly watches. His sacrifice and the story of the trek convince the king, and Gethen joins the Ekumen. Genly visits Estraven's family to bring them his journals.

=== BOOK: Foundation ===
# Foundation
by Isaac Asimov · Full story synopsis (spoilers)

The Galactic Empire has ruled for twelve thousand years, but the mathematician Hari Seldon has developed psychohistory, a science that predicts the future of huge populations. He calculates that the Empire will fall, followed by thirty thousand years of chaos. He believes he can reduce this to one thousand years by gathering the sum of human knowledge.

---

Seldon is put on trial for spreading panic. He bargains: he and his followers will be exiled to a remote planet, Terminus, to write the Encyclopedia Galactica. In reality, there is a second foundation elsewhere, but Terminus is the first, and its people do not know the full plan.

---

Years later, as outer provinces break away from the Empire, Terminus is threatened by hostile neighbours. The mayor, Salvor Hardin, realises the Encyclopedists cannot save themselves by force. Using shrewd politics and the planet's superior technology, he turns science into a religion among the neighbouring kingdoms, so they will not attack.

---

In later crises, merchants such as Hober Mallow expand the Foundation's power through trade instead of war, selling technology to other worlds and building dependence. Each time a crisis comes, Seldon's recorded hologram appears on schedule and shows that the solution was already predicted. The book ends with the Foundation growing from a small outpost into the seed of a new galactic civilisation.

=== BOOK: Guns, Germs, and Steel ===
# Guns, Germs, and Steel
by Jared Diamond · Summary of the argument

The book begins with a question from a New Guinean politician named Yali: why did Europeans end up with so much "cargo", the technology and wealth, while his people had so little? Diamond argues the answer is not biology or intelligence, but geography and environment.

---

Early human societies that lived in regions with many wild plants and animals suitable for domestication could farm and keep livestock. This produced surplus food, which supported larger populations, specialised jobs, writing, governments, and armies. The Fertile Crescent had many such species. Other regions, such as Australia and much of the Americas, had far fewer.

---

Eurasia also has a mostly east-west axis, so crops, animals and ideas could spread over similar climates. Africa and the Americas run north-south, which made spreading harder. Livestock also gave Eurasians diseases, such as smallpox, to which they developed immunity, and which later killed vast numbers of people in the Americas.

---

Diamond uses the 1532 meeting of Pizarro and the Inca emperor Atahualpa to illustrate his argument. A small band of Spaniards captured the emperor of millions, thanks to horses, steel weapons, guns, and above all the germs they carried. The book argues that inequality between peoples comes from the accidents of geography, not from the people themselves.

=== BOOK: SPQR ===
# SPQR
by Mary Beard · Summary of the book

SPQR stands for "Senatus Populusque Romanus", the Senate and People of Rome. Mary Beard tells the story of ancient Rome from its murky beginnings to the year 212 CE, when the emperor Caracalla granted citizenship to nearly all free men of the empire.

---

She begins with the foundation myths, such as Romulus and Remus, and shows that Romans themselves told conflicting versions of their early history. She treats the legends as clues to how Romans saw themselves. She then follows the growth of a small city-state into a republic with a Senate, magistrates, and armies that conquered Italy and then the Mediterranean.

---

Beard examines the crises of the late Republic: the rivalry of ambitious generals, the conspiracy of Catiline, which Cicero famously foiled, and the civil wars that followed. She shows how the old system strained under the weight of empire and ambition, and how Julius Caesar's rise and murder ended the Republic.

---

The book then turns to the age of emperors, beginning with Augustus, who disguised autocracy as a restored republic. Beard covers ordinary life as well as elites: slaves, freedmen, women, soldiers and provincials. Her central point is that Roman history is shaped by what we can and cannot know, and that Rome's greatest success was in making conquered people into Romans.

=== BOOK: The Guns of August ===
# The Guns of August
by Barbara W. Tuchman · Summary of the book

The book covers the first month of the First World War, in August 1914, and the decisions that led to it. Tuchman opens with the funeral of King Edward VII in 1910, where the rulers of Europe gathered for what would be the last time before the continent tore itself apart.

---

She explains the plans each nation had prepared. Germany's Schlieffen Plan called for a quick sweep through neutral Belgium to knock out France before Russia could mobilise. France planned a headlong offensive, relying on attack and spirit. Russia hurried its vast but poorly supplied armies toward Germany. Britain debated whether to join, until the invasion of Belgium made up its mind.

---

When war began, the plans collided with reality. The Belgians resisted longer than expected, and the German advance was delayed. German forces were slowed by the logistics of moving millions, and their commanders hesitated at crucial moments. In the east, Russia's armies suffered a crushing defeat at Tannenberg.

---

The turning point came at the First Battle of the Marne in September, when French and British forces halted the German advance on Paris. Both sides then dug in, and the war of movement became a war of trenches that would last four years. Tuchman shows how pride, rigid plans, and miscalculation by leaders created a catastrophe none of them wanted.

=== BOOK: Sapiens ===
# Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind
by Yuval Noah Harari · Summary of the argument

Harari tells the history of our species in three great revolutions. The first, the Cognitive Revolution around seventy thousand years ago, gave Homo sapiens the ability to talk about things that do not exist: gods, nations, money, laws. These shared fictions let huge numbers of strangers cooperate, which no other animal can do.

---

The second, the Agricultural Revolution roughly twelve thousand years ago, saw humans settle down to farm. Harari calls it "history's biggest fraud": farming produced more food and more people, but harder lives, worse health and new hierarchies, while the wheat, in a sense, domesticated humans.

---

Over time, humans built empires, trade networks and religions, all of which unified diverse peoples. Money, empires and universal religions are the three great forces that bound the world together. Harari argues they work because enough people believe in them.

---

The third, the Scientific Revolution about five hundred years ago, began when Europeans admitted their own ignorance and started to explore, measure and invent. Combined with capitalism and empire, this led to the modern world. Harari closes by asking where this is leading us, with biotechnology and artificial intelligence, and whether becoming more powerful has made us any happier.

=== BOOK: The Power of Being Alone ===
# The Power of Being Alone
An original short story

Amara had spent so many years being useful to other people that she no longer knew what she sounded like when she was quiet.

Her phone buzzed with a message from her sister: "Are you sure about this? A whole week in a cabin by yourself?" She typed back, "I'm sure," though she was not, and she switched the phone to silent before she could be talked out of it.

---

The cabin sat at the edge of a lake, with one room, a narrow bed, a wood stove, and a table that looked out on the water. The first evening felt like standing in an empty house after a party. Without the usual noise, her thoughts rushed in: the unanswered emails, the argument with her manager, the thing she had said to a friend two years ago and never apologised for. She lay awake until the stove ticked itself cold.

---

On the second day she walked the whole shore and did not talk to a soul. By the third day she had stopped checking her wrist for a watch she was not wearing. She noticed she was hungry before noon and ate when she wanted to. She noticed that she hummed when she cooked. She noticed that the lake changed colour three times a day, silver in the morning, green at noon, and a deep, quiet blue at dusk.

---

On the fourth day it rained, and she sat at the table with a notebook. She had meant to make a list of everything she had to do when she went home. Instead she found herself writing a list of everything she wanted. It was shorter than she expected, and stranger. Learn to swim properly. Say no more often. Stop answering every message at once. Write to the friend.

---

On the fifth day she wrote the apology. It took four drafts, and none of them was perfect. She did not send it, because there was no signal, and she was surprised to find that she did not feel any urgency to. She felt something larger, a quiet certainty that she was the kind of person who would send it.

---

On the sixth evening she sat on the dock with her feet in the cold water and listened to the loons calling across the lake. She understood that she had not been running away from anyone. She had been coming back to someone, a person she had left waiting for a long time.

On the last morning she packed slowly. She turned her phone back on and watched the messages arrive in a flood. Her sister had written: "You okay? Call me." Amara called, and when her sister answered, she laughed and said, "I am. I really am. Let me tell you about the lake."

She understood now that being alone was not the opposite of being close to people. It was how she learned to arrive among them as herself.
